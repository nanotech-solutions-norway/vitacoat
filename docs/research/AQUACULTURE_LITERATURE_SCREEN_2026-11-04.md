## Common active-component connection — operator clarification (04.11.2026)

The responsible product operator confirms **Citrox** is the antimicrobial active component used in VitaCoat's SiO₂-based coating matrix, and identifies a common Citrox active lineage across the supplier's aquaculture/seafood research portfolio. The controlled VitaCoat technical report independently documents **Citrox bioflavonoid/organic-acid actives embedded in a silica-based binder**. The earlier screening therefore must not describe the studies as scientifically unrelated to VitaCoat: **they are relevant to understanding the active ingredient's potential antimicrobial mechanism**.

**Independent, direct Citrox ingredient research:** S. J. Hooper, M. A. O. Lewis, M. J. Wilson & D. W. Williams (2011), “[Antimicrobial activity of Citrox bioflavonoid preparations against oral microorganisms](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.bdj.2010.1224),” *British Dental Journal* **210**, E22, reported antimicrobial activity in vitro for the **BC30/MDC30** Citrox preparations. This is ingredient/formulation-level research, not a test of a VitaCoat-coated surface.

**Research traceability caveat:** The other referenced studies name **Auranta 3009** and **AuraAqua** and describe mixed organic-acid/citrus-extract compositions rather than individually verified Citrox coating batches. The operator's common-active confirmation is recorded; the precise proprietary Citrox complex, concentration and formulation/batch mapping in each published study remains subject to source-owner confirmation. Reported effects of combinations cannot automatically be attributed solely to Citrox.

**Product-system distinction:** The ingredient connection supports the scientific rationale of VitaCoat, but antimicrobial reduction, pathogen spectrum, contact time, surface durability and duration for the **finished encapsulated coating** must be supported by the coating's separate, exact sample-specific laboratory evidence. Neither direct seafood treatment claims nor automatically transferred experimental aquaculture mortality figures are authorized.

## Verified bibliography and research limits

1. **Pinkerton, L., et al. (2019).** “Attenuation of *Vibrio parahaemolyticus* Virulence Factors by a Mixture of Natural Antimicrobials.” *Microorganisms* **7**(12), 679. DOI: [10.3390/microorganisms7120679](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms7120679). The investigated material was **Auranta 3009**. Its controlled shrimp challenge and fish-cell assays do not establish VitaCoat product performance. Some experimental timing details warrant source-method confirmation.
2. **Corcionivoschi, N., et al. (2024).** “Blends of Organic Acids Are Weaponizing the Host iNOS and Nitric Oxide to Reduce Infection of *Piscirickettsia salmonis* in vitro.” *Antioxidants* **13**(5), 542. DOI: [10.3390/antiox13050542](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox13050542). Final publication date **28 April 2024**. Research uses an **AuraAqua** blend in an in-vitro fish-cell infection model, not a VitaCoat surface-coating test or salmon farm trial. Earlier prepublication PDF versions contain placeholder journal metadata.
3. **Pet, I., et al. (2025).** “Shrimp White Spot Viral Infections Are Attenuated by Organic Acids by Regulating the Expression of HO-1 Oxygenase and β-1,3-Glucan-Binding Protein.” *Antioxidants* **14**(1), 89. DOI: [10.3390/antiox14010089](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox14010089). Published **14 January 2025**. Investigates **AuraAqua** under experimental WSSV-challenge conditions. It does **not** prove virucidal efficacy of VitaCoat as a hard-surface coating or permit aquaculture use.
